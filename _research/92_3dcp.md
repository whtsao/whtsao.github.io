<p style="text-align: justify;">
This project will advance underwater 3D concrete printing (3DCP) technologies through the development of a high-fidelity two-phase Newtonian-rheological flow model to simulate interactions between freshly extruded concrete and ambient water currents. 3DCP has rapidly emerged as a transformative technology in global construction, especially for the fabrication of freeform structures with reduced labor and material cost. However, its application in coastal and marine environments remains limited. In Louisiana, where critical infrastructure is surrounded by water, underwater 3DCP could support rapid post-disaster repair, ecological restoration, and protective coastal construction, yet the fundamental hydrodynamic mechanisms governing print stability are still poorly understood.
In this project, we will characterize two-phase flow interactions, where the fresh concrete and water are modeled as a Bingham fluid and a Newtonian fluid, respectively. The numerical module will be calibrated by rheological experiments, and then integrated into an open-source platform to extend its capability for simulating underwater 3DCP. We will further validate the numerical model through scaled experiments. The outcomes will establish safe operational conditions for underwater 3DCP, quantify relationships between rheological and environmental parameters, and deliver a computational tool for the assessment of buildability and structural integrity.
This work directly supports the FIRST Louisiana 2030 priorities in Advanced Manufacturing, Structural Integrity Assurance, and Resilience & Disaster Recovery. The results will advance resilient and sustainable coastal infrastructure and strengthen Louisiana’s leadership in innovative water-based construction.
</p>
